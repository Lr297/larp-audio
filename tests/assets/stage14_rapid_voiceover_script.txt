Consider a short product demonstration. The presenter opens the package, removes the item, and shows the main control. Each action can become a focused visual moment. The subtitle rhythm should support those moments without inventing new copy. One, two, or three words can form each rapid block.

The application runs on the user's computer. It does not send the script to a cloud service, and it does not upload the audio. Technical reports should explain what happened without exposing private absolute paths. Exported files remain portable, reviewable, and tied to one local processing run.

Performance matters during creative work. A subtitle stage should finish quickly enough that it does not feel stalled. The algorithm considers only local groups of up to three words. It stores compact costs and backpointers, then reconstructs the chosen sequence once. This keeps growth close to linear.

Validation matters too. Every block must contain at least one word and no more than three. Every script word appears once, in order. Punctuation stays attached to the preceding text. Cleaned timestamps remain monotonic. Repeated runs with identical inputs produce identical subtitle files.
